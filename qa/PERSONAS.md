# Personas – Loaded Out

## nina: Nina Brunner
Role: user
Device: phone
Languages: en
Never does: J4, J5
Nina, 27, works shifts in a pharmacy and wants to lose about six kilos before summer; this is her first fitness app. She uses her phone one-handed on the tram, skims instead of reading, and taps the first button that looks right. She logs food and the odd workout, but money and pantry tracking feel like homework to her. If a screen asks too much at once, or something fails without saying why, she closes the app and doesn't come back.

## jonas: Jonas Meier
Role: user
Device: phone
Languages: en
Never does: J4, J5
Jonas, 34, is a software tester who has lifted four times a week for ten years and runs a push/pull/legs split. He builds detailed templates, types exact weights and reps between sets, and checks that the volume and personal records add up. He is fast and confident with apps and expects things to work on the first try. Losing a logged set, a duplicated workout or a total that doesn't match his own maths makes him stop trusting the app.

## lea: Lea Hofmann
Role: user
Device: phone
Languages: en
Never does: J2
Lea, 21, studies in Zurich and lives on a monthly allowance of about CHF 1500. She cares more about groceries and money than about macros: she wants to plan cheap meals, keep track of what is in her kitchen and see what is left of her food budget. She is quick on her phone but impatient with anything that doesn't feel useful. If the budget numbers look wrong or the app shows a currency she didn't choose, she goes back to her spreadsheet.

## heinz: Heinz Graf
Role: user
Device: tablet
Languages: en
Never does: J2
Heinz, 67, is retired, and after a heart check-up his doctor asked him to watch his meals and keep a weekly routine. He uses a tablet his daughter set up, reads every word on a screen before he taps, and types slowly with one finger. He needs to see clearly that something was saved, and he worries when a tap gives no visible response. Small icon-only buttons, messages that vanish too fast, or anything that deletes without asking make him anxious, and he will give up and call his daughter.
